From Einstein's General Theory of Relativity, we know that there is no universal, fixed time shared by the entire universe.

Apart from the second law of thermodynamics, expressed as ΔS ≥ 0, we have no other fundamental physical laws that distinguish the past from the future.

According to Ludwig Boltzmann, the distinction between the past and the future arises from our blurred perception of the world.

Time does not possess an inherent direction. Rather, its direction emerges from our limited perspective. We tend to assume that our personal experience of reality reflects how the entire universe works.

Time has no absolute unity, direction, universal present, continuity, or independence.

**The world is a network of events.**

> "Nothing is: things happen."

> "The world is made up of networks of kisses, not of stones."

> "Things are, in themselves, events that for a while are monotonous."

> "Time temporalizes itself only to the extent that it is human."

**We are time.**
We are not things or substances, but events in a continuous process of becoming.
We are our own histories, shaped by everything that has happened to us.
We exist not merely in the present, but through the relationships between what has been and what is yet to come.
We are not simply experiencing time; we are part of its unfolding.
